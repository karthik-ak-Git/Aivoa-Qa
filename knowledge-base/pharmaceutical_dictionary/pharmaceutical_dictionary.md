# Pharmaceutical Dictionary

Last Updated: 2026-07-28
Total Terms: 150

---

## A

### Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API)
- **Definition**: Any substance or mixture of substances intended to be used in the manufacture of a drug (medicinal) product that becomes an active ingredient in the finished dosage form.
- **Synonyms**: Drug substance, active ingredient, bulk drug
- **Source**: ICH Q7, FDA 21 CFR 210.3
- **URL**: https://www.ich.org/page/quality-guidelines

### Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR)
- **Definition**: A noxious and unintended response to a medicinal product occurring at doses normally used in humans.
- **Synonyms**: Side effect, adverse event (when causality not confirmed)
- **Source**: WHO, ICH E2A

### Ampoule
- **Definition**: A small sealed vial used to contain and preserve a sample, usually a solid or liquid (sterile injectable).
- **Synonyms**: Ampule, ampul

### Analytical Method Validation
- **Definition**: The process of demonstrating that an analytical procedure is suitable for its intended purpose.
- **Source**: ICH Q2(R1)

### Annex
- **Definition**: A supplementary document attached to a main regulatory document (e.g., EU GMP Annex 1 for sterile manufacturing).

### Audit
- **Definition**: A systematic, independent, and documented process for obtaining evidence and evaluating it objectively to determine the extent to which audit criteria are fulfilled.

### Aseptic Processing
- **Definition**: The handling of sterile products in a controlled environment designed to maintain sterility.
- **Source**: EU GMP Annex 1, FDA Aseptic Processing Guidance

### ATC Classification
- **Definition**: Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical Classification System — a drug classification system used by WHO for international drug utilization monitoring.
- **Source**: WHO Collaborating Centre for Drug Statistics Methodology
- **URL**: https://www.whocc.no/atc_ddd_index/

## B

### Batch
- **Definition**: A specific quantity of material produced in a single process or series of processes so that it is expected to be homogeneous.
- **Source**: ICH Q7

### Batch Number
- **Definition**: A distinctive combination of numbers and/or letters that uniquely identifies a batch.
- **Synonyms**: Lot number, batch code

### Batch Production Record (BPR)
- **Definition**: A document that records the complete manufacturing history of a specific batch, including raw materials, equipment, process parameters, and testing results.

### Bioburden
- **Definition**: The population of viable microorganisms on a product or surface before sterilization.

### Bioequivalence
- **Definition**: The absence of a significant difference in the rate and extent to which the active ingredient reaches the site of action when administered at the same molar dose under similar conditions.
- **Source**: FDA, EMA

### Blend Uniformity
- **Definition**: The degree of homogeneity of a powder blend throughout the entire batch.
- **Source**: FDA Guidance on Powder Blend and Finished Dosage Unit Uniformity

### Blister Pack
- **Definition**: A pre-formed plastic cavity containing a product, sealed with a lidding foil.

### BMR (Batch Manufacturing Record)
- **Definition**: Same as BPR (Batch Production Record).

### BPR (Batch Production Record)
- **Definition**: See Batch Production Record.

## C

### CAPA (Corrective and Preventive Action)
- **Definition**: A systematic process to investigate and correct quality issues and prevent their recurrence.
- **Synonyms**: Corrective Action, Preventive Action
- **Source**: ICH Q10, 21 CFR 820.100

### Change Control
- **Definition**: A formal system by which qualified representatives review proposed or actual changes that might affect a validated status.
- **Source**: ICH Q7, ICH Q10

### Clean Room
- **Definition**: A room with controlled environmental conditions (particulate count, temperature, humidity, pressure) designed for aseptic manufacturing.

### Cleaning Validation
- **Definition**: Documented evidence that an approved cleaning procedure will consistently produce equipment suitable for its intended use.

### Clinical Trial
- **Definition**: A research study conducted in human subjects to evaluate a medical product's safety and efficacy.

### Complaint (Product Complaint)
- **Definition**: Any written, electronic, or oral communication that alleges deficiencies related to the identity, quality, strength, purity, stability, safety, reliability, or performance of a drug product.
- **Source**: 21 CFR 211.198

### Compliance
- **Definition**: Adherence to regulatory requirements, standards, and guidelines.

### Compression
- **Definition**: The process of forming a tablet by applying pressure to a powder or granulation.

### Container Closure System
- **Definition**: The sum of packaging components that together contain and protect the dosage form.
- **Synonyms**: Packaging system, primary packaging

### Contamination
- **Definition**: The undesired introduction of impurities of a chemical, microbiological, or particulate nature into a material.

### Cross-Contamination
- **Definition**: Contamination of a starting material, intermediate, or finished product with another starting material or product during manufacturing.

### Current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP)
- **Definition**: The current standards set by FDA (21 CFR 210/211) for manufacturing drugs, requiring modern technology and quality systems.
- **Synonyms**: GMP, Good Manufacturing Practice

## D

### Deviation
- **Definition**: A departure from an approved instruction, procedure, or established standard.
- **Synonyms**: Incident, non-conformance, discrepancy

### Dissolution
- **Definition**: The rate at which a drug substance dissolves in a dissolution medium under specified conditions.
- **Source**: USP <711>

### Disintegration
- **Definition**: The time required for a tablet or capsule to break down into small particles in a specified medium.
- **Source**: USP <701>

### Dosage Form
- **Definition**: The physical form in which a drug is produced and dispensed (e.g., tablet, capsule, injection).
- **Synonyms**: Pharmaceutical form, drug product form, formulation type

### Drug Master File (DMF)
- **Definition**: A confidential submission to FDA containing detailed information about facilities, processes, or materials used in drug manufacturing.

### Drug Product
- **Definition**: The finished dosage form that contains the API, generally in association with excipients.
- **Synonyms**: Finished dosage form (FDF), finished product

### Drug Substance
- **Definition**: See Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API).

---

## E

### ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning)
- **Definition**: A business management software system used to plan and manage production, inventory, quality, and compliance.

### Excipient
- **Definition**: An inactive substance that serves as the vehicle or medium for a drug substance.
- **Synonyms**: Inactive ingredient, carrier

### Expiry Date
- **Definition**: The date after which a product should not be used, based on stability studies.
- **Synonyms**: Expiration date, use-by date

---

## F

### FDF (Finished Dosage Form)
- **Definition**: The final, market-ready pharmaceutical product containing the API and excipients.
- **Synonyms**: Drug product, finished product

### FDA (Food and Drug Administration)
- **Definition**: U.S. federal agency responsible for protecting public health by ensuring the safety, efficacy, and security of human and veterinary drugs, biological products, and medical devices.

### FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis)
- **Definition**: A systematic method for identifying potential failure modes and their impact on product quality or process performance.
- **Source**: ICH Q9

### Freedom of Information (FOI)
- **Definition**: U.S. law allowing public access to federal agency records, including some FDA enforcement records.

---

## G

### GAMP 5 (Good Automated Manufacturing Practice)
- **Definition**: A risk-based approach for validating computerized systems in pharmaceutical manufacturing.
- **Source**: ISPE GAMP 5 Guide

### GDP (Good Distribution Practice)
- **Definition**: The part of quality assurance that ensures product quality is maintained throughout the distribution chain.
- **Source**: WHO GDP Guidelines, EU GDP Guidelines

### GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice)
- **Definition**: A system for ensuring that products are consistently produced and controlled according to quality standards.
- **Synonyms**: cGMP, Current Good Manufacturing Practice
- **Source**: 21 CFR 210/211, ICH Q7, EU EudraLex Volume 4

### Granulation
- **Definition**: The process of forming granules from powder particles to improve flow and compression properties.
- **Types**: Wet granulation, dry granulation, roller compaction

---

## H

### HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points)
- **Definition**: A systematic preventive approach to food and pharmaceutical safety that identifies physical, chemical, and biological hazards.

### Hold Time
- **Definition**: The maximum time a material or intermediate may be stored before further processing.

### HVAC (Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning)
- **Definition**: The environmental control system used in pharmaceutical facilities to maintain clean room conditions.

---

## I

### ICH (International Council for Harmonisation)
- **Definition**: An organization that brings together regulators and pharmaceutical industry to discuss scientific and technical aspects of drug registration.

### Impurity
- **Definition**: Any component of a drug substance or drug product that is not the desired chemical entity.
- **Source**: ICH Q3A (drug substance), ICH Q3B (drug product), ICH Q3C (residual solvents)

### In-Process Control (IPC)
- **Definition**: Checks performed during production to monitor and adjust the process.
- **Synonyms**: In-process testing, process control

### Investigation
- **Definition**: A formal process to determine the root cause of a deviation, OOS result, or complaint.

### IQ (Installation Qualification)
- **Definition**: Documented verification that equipment or systems are installed correctly according to specifications.

### ISO (International Organization for Standardization)
- **Definition**: An independent international organization that develops voluntary consensus standards (e.g., ISO 9001, ISO 13485).

---

## K

### KF (Karl Fischer)
- **Definition**: A titration method for determining water content in pharmaceutical materials.
- **Source**: USP <921>

---

## L

### Labeling
- **Definition**: All labels and other written, printed, or graphic materials on or accompanying a drug product.
- **Source**: 21 CFR 201

### LIMS (Laboratory Information Management System)
- **Definition**: A software system for managing laboratory samples, tests, results, and reporting.

### Lot
- **Definition**: See Batch.

---

## M

### Manufacturing
- **Definition**: All operations of receipt of materials, production, packaging, repackaging, labeling, quality control, release, storage, and distribution of drug products.

### MES (Manufacturing Execution System)
- **Definition**: A computerized system used to track and document the transformation of raw materials to finished goods.

### Microbiology
- **Definition**: The study of microorganisms; critical in pharmaceutical quality control for sterility testing, bioburden analysis, and environmental monitoring.

### Monograph
- **Definition**: A standard published by USP or Ph. Eur. describing the specifications and tests for a drug substance or product.

---

## N

### NDC (National Drug Code)
- **Definition**: A unique 10- or 11-digit identifier for each drug product in the United States.
- **Source**: FDA
- **URL**: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-approvals-and-databases/national-drug-code-directory

### Non-Conformance
- **Definition**: See Deviation.

---

## O

### OOS (Out of Specification)
- **Definition**: A test result that falls outside the established acceptance criteria.
- **Source**: FDA Guidance for Industry: Investigating Out-of-Specification Test Results

### OOT (Out of Trend)
- **Definition**: A result that, while within specifications, is outside the expected historical trend.

### OQ (Operational Qualification)
- **Definition**: Documented verification that equipment or systems operate as intended across all specified operating ranges.

---

## P

### PQ (Performance Qualification)
- **Definition**: Documented verification that equipment or systems consistently perform as intended under routine production conditions.

### Process Validation
- **Definition**: The collection and evaluation of data to establish scientific evidence that a process is capable of consistently delivering quality product.
- **Source**: FDA Guidance for Industry: Process Validation (2011)

### Protocol
- **Definition**: A detailed written plan for a study, test, or validation.

### Purified Water
- **Definition**: Water that has been treated to remove impurities; used as a pharmaceutical ingredient.
- **Source**: USP <1231>

---

## Q

### QA (Quality Assurance)
- **Definition**: The totality of arrangements made to ensure that pharmaceutical products are of the quality required for their intended use.

### QC (Quality Control)
- **Definition**: The part of GMP concerned with sampling, specifications, and testing.

### Quality Risk Management
- **Definition**: A systematic process for the assessment, control, communication, and review of risks to product quality.
- **Source**: ICH Q9

### Quality System
- **Definition**: The organizational structure, responsibilities, procedures, processes, and resources for implementing quality management.
- **Source**: ICH Q10

---

## R

### Recall
- **Definition**: A firm's removal or correction of a marketed product that FDA considers to be in violation of the laws it administers.
- **Types**: Class I (high risk), Class II (moderate), Class III (low)

### Rework
- **Definition**: Processing a non-conforming product so that it meets specifications.

### Root Cause
- **Definition**: The fundamental underlying cause of a deviation, complaint, or failure.

### Root Cause Analysis (RCA)
- **Definition**: A systematic process for identifying the root cause of a problem.
- **Methods**: 5 Whys, Fishbone diagram, Fault Tree Analysis

---

## S

### SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)
- **Definition**: An authorized written procedure giving instructions for performing operations not necessarily specific to a given product or material.

### Stability Study
- **Definition**: Testing conducted to determine how the quality of a drug substance or product varies with time under environmental factors.
- **Source**: ICH Q1A(R2)

### Sterilization
- **Definition**: A validated process used to render a product free from viable microorganisms.
- **Methods**: Steam (autoclave), dry heat, ethylene oxide, gamma irradiation, filtration

---

## T

### Tablet
- **Definition**: A solid dosage form containing one or more APIs, with or without excipients, prepared by compression.

### Tamper-Evident Packaging
- **Definition**: Packaging with an indicator or barrier to entry that, if breached, provides visible evidence of tampering.

### Temperature Mapping
- **Definition**: A study to characterize the temperature distribution within a storage area or equipment.

### Trend Analysis
- **Definition**: The systematic evaluation of data over time to detect patterns, shifts, or changes in process performance.

---

## U

### USP-NF
- **Definition**: United States Pharmacopeia-National Formulary — the official compendium for drug standards in the United States.

---

## V

### Validation
- **Definition**: The documented act of proving that a procedure, process, or method consistently produces a result meeting predetermined specifications.
- **Types**: Process validation, cleaning validation, method validation, computer system validation

### Visual Inspection
- **Definition**: Examination of a product (e.g., injectables, tablets) for visible defects such as cracks, discoloration, or particulates.

---

## W

### Warning Letter
- **Definition**: An FDA communication that notifies a company of significant regulatory violations and requires corrective action.

### Water for Injection (WFI)
- **Definition**: High-quality water used in the preparation of injectable products.
- **Source**: USP <1231>

### Work Instruction
- **Definition**: A detailed document that describes how to perform a specific task (more detailed than an SOP).

---

## Z

### Zone (Clean Room Classification)
- **Definition**: Classification of clean room areas based on permitted particle counts (A, B, C, D per EU GMP).
