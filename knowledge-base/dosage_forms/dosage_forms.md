## 1. Tablet (Compressed)
- **Definition**: A solid dosage form containing one or more APIs, with or without excipients, prepared by compression.
- **Synonyms**: Pill, tab, compressed tablet
- **Manufacturing Process**: Granulation (wet/dry) → Blending → Compression → (Optional: Coating)
- **Common Quality Issues**: Weight variation, hardness variation, friability, capping, lamination, sticking, picking
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Broken tablets, chipped tablets, discolored tablets, hardness issues, slow dissolution
- **Packaging**: Bottles (HDPE), blisters (PVC/Alu), unit dose pouches
- **Sub-types**:
  - Immediate Release (IR)
  - Extended Release (ER/XR/CR)
  - Orally Disintegrating (ODT)
  - Chewable
  - Effervescent
  - Film-coated
  - Sugar-coated
  - Enteric-coated
- **USP Reference**: <701> Disintegration, <711> Dissolution, <1216> Tablet Friability
- **Sources**: USP, FDA, ICH Q6A

## 2. Capsule (Hard Gelatin)
- **Definition**: A solid dosage form in which the drug is enclosed within a hard gelatin or HPMC shell.
- **Synonyms**: Cap, hard capsule, two-piece capsule
- **Manufacturing Process**: Shell preparation → Powder blending → Encapsulation → (Optional: Banding/sealing)
- **Common Quality Issues**: Weight variation, disintegration failure, cross-linking (gelatin), shell brittleness
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Empty capsules, stuck capsules, broken shells, powder leakage
- **Packaging**: Bottles, blisters
- **Sub-types**: Hard gelatin, HPMC (vegetarian), delayed-release, extended-release
- **USP Reference**: <701> Disintegration, <711> Dissolution

## 3. Capsule (Soft Gelatin)
- **Definition**: A one-piece, sealed soft gelatin shell containing liquid or semi-solid fill.
- **Synonyms**: Softgel, soft capsule, soft elastic capsule
- **Manufacturing Process**: Gel mass preparation → Rotary die encapsulation → Drying → Inspection
- **Common Quality Issues**: Leakage, deformation, shell hardening, cross-linking, fill discoloration
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Leaking capsules, stuck together, soft/hard shells, odor
- **Packaging**: Bottles, blisters

## 4. Injection (Liquid)
- **Definition**: A sterile, pyrogen-free liquid preparation intended for parenteral administration.
- **Synonyms**: Injectable, parenteral, shot, IV/IM/SC solution
- **Manufacturing Process**: Formulation → Filtration → Filling → Sterilization (terminal or aseptic) → Inspection
- **Common Quality Issues**: Particulates, visible particles, endotoxins, sterility failure, pH shift
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Particles visible, discoloration, leaking vial, cracked ampoule, pain on injection
- **Packaging**: Vials, ampoules, prefilled syringes, cartridges, IV bags
- **Sub-types**: Solution, suspension, emulsion, reconstitutable powder
- **Manufacturing Environment**: Class A (ISO 5) or better under EU GMP Annex 1

## 5. Syrup
- **Definition**: An oral liquid preparation containing the API dissolved in a concentrated sugar solution.
- **Synonyms**: Oral solution, liquid oral
- **Manufacturing Process**: Dissolution → Mixing → Filtration → Filling → Sealing
- **Common Quality Issues**: Microbial growth, crystallization, color change, pH drift
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Mold/visible growth, discoloration, sediment, bad taste
- **Packaging**: Glass or plastic bottles with child-resistant closures

## 6. Suspension
- **Definition**: A liquid preparation containing solid particles dispersed in a liquid vehicle.
- **Manufacturing Process**: Wetting → Dispersion → Homogenization → Filling
- **Common Quality Issues**: Sedimentation, caking, particle size growth, microbial contamination
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Cannot re-suspend, clumping, uneven dosing, discoloration
- **Packaging**: Bottles (shake well required)

## 7. Cream
- **Definition**: A semisolid emulsion of oil and water for external application.
- **Synonyms**: Topical cream, dermatological cream
- **Manufacturing Process**: Oil phase + Water phase preparation → Emulsification → Cooling → Homogenization → Filling
- **Common Quality Issues**: Phase separation, microbial contamination, viscosity change, grainy texture
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Separation, gritty texture, discoloration, bad odor, tube bursting
- **Packaging**: Tubes, jars, pump bottles

## 8. Ointment
- **Definition**: A semisolid preparation containing the API in a greasy base for external application.
- **Synonyms**: Pomade, unguent
- **Manufacturing Process**: Base melting → API incorporation → Homogenization → Filling
- **Common Quality Issues**: Grittiness, separation, viscosity variation
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Greasy residue, too thick/thin, separation
- **Packaging**: Tubes, jars

## 9. Gel
- **Definition**: A semisolid system consisting of a liquid phase gelled with a gelling agent.
- **Synonyms**: Jelly, hydrogel, gel formulation
- **Common Quality Issues**: Syneresis (liquid separation), microbial contamination, viscosity loss
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Watery, dried out, sticky, discoloration
- **Packaging**: Tubes, pump bottles

## 10. Transdermal Patch
- **Definition**: A medicated adhesive patch placed on the skin to deliver a dose of medication through the skin.
- **Synonyms**: Patch, transdermal delivery system (TDS), skin patch
- **Manufacturing Process**: Drug layer preparation → Lamination → Die-cutting → Pouch sealing
- **Common Quality Issues**: Adhesion failure, release liner issues, drug crystallization, skin irritation
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Won't stick, falls off, skin reaction, too strong/weak, edge lifting
- **Packaging**: Individual sealed pouches

## 11. Eye Drops
- **Definition**: A sterile liquid or suspension for instillation into the eye.
- **Synonyms**: Ophthalmic solution, eye drops
- **Manufacturing Process**: Aseptic compounding → Filtration → Sterile filling → Inspection
- **Common Quality Issues**: Sterility breaches, particulate matter, pH shift, preservative failure
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Stinging, cloudy solution, leaking bottle, broken dropper
- **Packaging**: Sterile dropper bottles, single-dose units

## 12. Inhaler
- **Definition**: A device that delivers medication through the lungs.
- **Types**: Metered-dose inhaler (MDI), dry powder inhaler (DPI), soft mist inhaler (SMI)
- **Manufacturing Process**: (MDI) Suspension/solution preparation → Can filling → Valve insertion → Testing
- **Common Quality Issues**: Dose inconsistency, valve malfunction, clogging, propellant leakage
- **Typical Complaint Types**: No mist/spray, low dose, clogged, counter not working
- **Packaging**: Canister in actuator with dust cap

## 13. Nasal Spray
- **Definition**: A liquid preparation for administration into the nostrils.
- **Manufacturing Process**: Formulation → Filling into bottle → Pump/actuator assembly
- **Common Quality Issues**: Pump failure, clogging, dose uniformity, microbial contamination
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Spray not working, weak spray, clogged, dripping out
- **Packaging**: Bottles with metered-dose pump and actuator

## 14. Suppository
- **Definition**: A solid dosage form intended for insertion into the rectum or vagina.
- **Manufacturing Process**: Base melting → API dispersion → Molding → Cooling → Wrapping
- **Common Quality Issues**: Softening, melting point shift, cracking, discoloration
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Too soft, melted in package, too hard to insert, staining
- **Packaging**: Individual foil or plastic wrappers

## 15. Powder (Oral)
- **Definition**: A dry preparation of finely divided drug particles for oral administration.
- **Types**: Bulk powder, divided powder, powder for reconstitution, effervescent powder
- **Common Quality Issues**: Moisture absorption, caking, particle size variation
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Clumping, difficult to dissolve, bad taste
- **Packaging**: Sachets, bottles, stick packs

## 16. Drops (Oral)
- **Definition**: A liquid preparation administered in small volumes using a dropper.
- **Manufacturing Process**: Dissolution → Filtration → Filling with dropper assembly
- **Common Quality Issues**: Crystallization, microbial contamination, dropper inaccuracy
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Dropper not working, uneven drops, discoloration
- **Packaging**: Bottles with dropper

## 17. Infusion
- **Definition**: A sterile liquid preparation administered intravenously over a period of time.
- **Synonyms**: IV infusion, drip
- **Manufacturing Process**: Formulation → Sterile filtration → Filling → Terminal sterilization → Inspection
- **Common Quality Issues**: Endotoxin, particulates, sterility failure, container leaks
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Cloudy solution, particles, leaking bag, wrong volume
- **Packaging**: IV bags, plastic bottles, glass bottles
- **Volume**: Typically 50 mL to 1000 mL

## 18. Lotion
- **Definition**: A low-viscosity liquid preparation for external application.
- **Manufacturing Process**: Oil phase + Water phase → Emulsification → Homogenization → Filling
- **Common Quality Issues**: Phase separation, viscosity variation, microbial growth
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Watery, separation, pump not working
- **Packaging**: Bottles with pump, squeeze bottles

## 19. Oral Paste
- **Definition**: A semisolid preparation for oral application, typically on the gums or oral mucosa.
- **Examples**: Dental paste, oromucosal paste
- **Common Quality Issues**: Drying out, too stiff, tube bursting
- **Packaging**: Tubes

## 20. Lozenges
- **Definition**: A solid preparation containing one or more APIs intended to be dissolved or disintegrated in the mouth.
- **Synonyms**: Troche, pastille
- **Manufacturing Process**: Mixing → Molding or compression → Drying → Packaging
- **Common Quality Issues**: Hardness variation, dissolution issues, sticking in mold
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Too hard, too soft, crumbling, bad taste
- **Packaging**: Wrapped individually or in strips

## 21. Pessary
- **Definition**: A solid dosage form for insertion into the vagina.
- **Synonyms**: Vaginal suppository, vaginal tablet
- **Manufacturing Process**: Similar to suppository
- **Packaging**: Individual wrappers

## 22. Implant
- **Definition**: A sterile solid dosage form placed under the skin for prolonged drug release.
- **Manufacturing Process**: Sterile compression or molding → Aseptic packaging
- **Common Quality Issues**: Sterility failure, breakage during insertion
- **Packaging**: Sterile individual packaging

## 23. Inhalation Solution
- **Definition**: A liquid solution for administration via a nebulizer.
- **Manufacturing Process**: Aseptic formulation → Filling → Packaging
- **Common Quality Issues**: Sterility, pH, preservative efficacy
- **Packaging**: Single-dose ampoules or multi-dose bottles

## 24. Patch (Topical)
- **Definition**: A medicated adhesive patch for localized topical delivery (not transdermal).
- **Examples**: Lidocaine patch, diclofenac patch
- **Common Quality Issues**: Adhesion failure, skin irritation, edge lifting
- **Typical Complaint Types**: Won't stick, skin reaction
- **Packaging**: Individual pouches
